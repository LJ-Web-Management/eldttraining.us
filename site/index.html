What is ELDT training?

Quick answer

ELDT (Entry-Level Driver Training) is the FMCSA's federal minimum training standard for new commercial drivers. Since February 7, 2022, anyone getting a Class A or Class B CDL for the first time, upgrading from Class B to Class A, or adding a first-time Hazmat (H), Passenger (P), or School Bus (S) endorsement must complete training from a provider on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry before the state will let them test.

ELDT has two parts. Theory is classroom-style knowledge training that can be completed online; you must score at least 80% on the final assessment. Behind-the-wheel (BTW) training happens in a real truck or bus with a qualified instructor and is required for Class A, Class B, P, and S (not H). There is no minimum number of hours for either part. Your training provider reports each completed part to the Training Provider Registry, and your state driver licensing agency checks that record before your skills test (or before the H knowledge test).

FAQs

What is ELDT?

ELDT (Entry-Level Driver Training) is a set of federal training requirements established by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) in 49 CFR Part 380 for individuals seeking a commercial driver's license (CDL) or certain CDL endorsements for the first time. It took effect on February 7, 2022.

Who needs to complete ELDT?

ELDT applies to individuals who are:

  • Obtaining a Class A CDL for the first time
  • Obtaining a Class B CDL for the first time
  • Upgrading from a Class B to a Class A CDL
  • Obtaining a Passenger (P) endorsement for the first time
  • Obtaining a School Bus (S) endorsement for the first time
  • Obtaining a Hazardous Materials (H) endorsement for the first time
What training must an entry-level driver complete before the CDL skills or knowledge test?

To be eligible to take the Class A or Class B CDL skills test, the S or P skills test, or the H knowledge test, applicants must complete the applicable entry-level driver training from a registered training provider. Class A, Class B, P, and S require both theory and behind-the-wheel training; H requires theory only.

When did the ELDT requirements take effect?

The federal ELDT requirements took effect on February 7, 2022. They are not retroactive: drivers who already held the applicable CDL or endorsement before that date do not need ELDT for it.

Can I complete ELDT training online?

Yes, for the theory portion. Theory may be completed online with any provider listed on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR). For Class A, Class B, P, and S, behind-the-wheel training must still be completed in a vehicle with a qualified, TPR-registered instructor.

How fast is my completion reported to the FMCSA?

Federal rules give providers until midnight of the second business day after you finish. We submit your completion to the Training Provider Registry as soon as you pass the final theory assessment with 80% or higher.
