Class B ELDT Training Online (FMCSA-Compliant Theory Course)

This is the federally required ELDT theory course for a Class B commercial driver's license. Anyone applying for a Class B CDL for the first time must complete Class B theory with a provider on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry before taking the Class B skills test.

It covers the Class B theory curriculum in Appendix B to 49 CFR Part 380: basic operation, safe operating procedures, advanced operating practices, vehicle systems and malfunctions, and non-driving activities such as hours of service, cargo, and post-crash procedures. Who needs this course?

  • First-time Class B CDL applicants driving single vehicles with a GVWR of 26,001 lbs or more (straight trucks, dump trucks, box trucks, cement mixers, garbage trucks)
  • Future city bus, transit, and motorcoach drivers (add the Passenger endorsement course)
  • Future school bus drivers (add the Passenger and School Bus endorsement courses)
  • Municipal, utility, and construction employees whose employer requires a Class B CDL

You do not need this course if: Drivers who already held a Class B CDL before February 7, 2022, or anyone going for a Class A CDL (Class A training also qualifies you to test for Class B).

Frequently asked questions

Is Class B ELDT different from Class A ELDT?

Yes. FMCSA publishes separate theory curricula for Class A (Appendix A) and Class B (Appendix B). Class B omits combination-vehicle topics such as coupling and uncoupling. If you complete Class A ELDT you may take the Class B skills test, but Class B ELDT does not qualify you for Class A.

Do bus drivers need Class B ELDT?

Usually yes. Most transit and school buses require a Class B CDL plus the Passenger (P) endorsement, and school bus drivers also need the School Bus (S) endorsement. Each of those requires its own ELDT theory course.

How long does the Class B course take?

About 13 hours across 29 lessons, self-paced. There is no federal minimum number of theory hours; you must score 80% or higher on the final assessment.

Do I still need behind-the-wheel training?

Yes. Class B applicants must complete range and public-road BTW training with a TPR-registered provider before the skills test. This course covers the theory portion only.

I already have my learner’s permit in hand. Do I need to take more tests in this course, or is it just a precursor to take the test I have already passed?

If you already have your CDL learner’s permit in hand, then you have already passed the written permit tests required by your state. This ELDT course is not another DMV permit test. It is the federally required Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) theory training that must be completed and submitted to the FMCSA Training Provider Registry before you can take your CDL behind-the-wheel skills test (pre-trip, backing, and road test). You will complete quizzes and a final exam within the course itself, but those are for training completion purposes only and are separate from the DMV permit exams you already passed.
