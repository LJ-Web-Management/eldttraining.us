The CDL Skills Test: Pre-Trip, Backing, and Road Test Explained

The three parts of the CDL skills test, what examiners look for, and how to avoid the most common automatic fails.

Updated November 16, 20263 min readReviewed by the ELDT Training curriculum team
Quick answer

The CDL skills test has three parts: (1) a vehicle inspection (pre-trip), (2) basic vehicle control (backing maneuvers), and (3) an on-road driving test, all in a vehicle representative of the license class you want. You can take it after holding your CLP for at least 14 days and only once your state confirms your ELDT theory and behind-the-wheel records in the FMCSA Training Provider Registry. Failing any part usually ends the test for that day.

Before the test: ELDT verification

For a first-time Class A or Class B CDL, a B-to-A upgrade, or a first-time P or S endorsement, the state must verify both ELDT theory and BTW records before administering the skills test. Make sure both providers have reported. See ELDT certificate.

Part 1: Vehicle inspection

You'll walk the examiner through inspecting the vehicle, naming each component and explaining what you're checking for. It typically includes:

  • Engine compartment (fluids, belts, hoses, leaks)
  • Cab check and engine start (gauges, warning lights, controls, emergency equipment)
  • In-cab air brake check (for air-brake vehicles)
  • External inspection: lights, steering, suspension, brakes, wheels and tires
  • Coupling system and trailer (Class A)
  • Bus-specific items (passenger and school bus tests)

Use our pre-trip inspection checklist to practice.

Part 2: Basic vehicle control

The examiner selects maneuvers from:

  • Straight-line backing
  • Offset backing (left or right)
  • Parallel parking (sight side or conventional/blind side)
  • Alley dock (90-degree backing)

You're scored on encroachments (touching or crossing boundaries), pull-ups (stopping and pulling forward to reposition), and final position. Getting out to look (GOAL) is allowed a limited number of times.

Part 3: Road test

You'll drive a set route covering turns, intersections, lane changes, highway merges, curves, railroad crossings, and possibly an upgrade/downgrade and a simulated stop. Examiners watch for:

  • Mirror checks and visual search
  • Proper signaling and lane position
  • Speed and following distance
  • Smooth shifting and braking
  • Safe turns without curb strikes
  • Full stops and correct railroad crossing procedure

Common reasons people fail

  • Forgetting steps of the in-cab air brake check
  • Too many pull-ups or encroachments in backing
  • Rolling stops, missed mirror checks, or curb strikes on turns
  • Not wearing a seat belt, or other serious safety errors (automatic fail)

Your test vehicle determines restrictions

Testing in an automatic transmission adds an E restriction; testing without air brakes adds L or Z; a Class A test in a combination without a fifth wheel adds O. See CDL restrictions.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the CDL skills test?

Usually 1.5 to 3 hours for all three parts, depending on the state and vehicle.

Can I take the skills test in another state?

Many states let you test where you trained and transfer results to your home state. Ask both states first.

What happens if I fail one part?

Typically the test stops, and you retest that part (or the whole test) later under your state's rules and fees. Your ELDT records stay valid in the registry.

Do I need my own truck for the skills test?

You need a representative vehicle that passes safety checks. BTW providers often supply one for testing.
