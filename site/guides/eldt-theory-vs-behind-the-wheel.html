ELDT Theory vs Behind-the-Wheel Training

ELDT has two halves. Here's exactly what each one covers, who needs both, and how to fit them together.

Updated November 16, 20264 min readReviewed by the ELDT Training curriculum team
Quick answer

ELDT theory is the knowledge portion (regulations, vehicle systems, safe operating practices) and can be completed online with an 80% passing score. Behind-the-wheel (BTW) training is hands-on driving in a real commercial vehicle with a qualified instructor. Class A, Class B, Passenger (P), and School Bus (S) require both. The Hazmat (H) endorsement requires theory only. Neither part has minimum hours, they can be done in any order, and they can come from different registered providers.

What ELDT theory covers

For Class A and Class B, theory follows five units:

  1. Basic operation: orientation, control systems and dashboard, pre- and post-trip inspections, basic control, shifting, backing and docking, and (Class A) coupling and uncoupling.
  2. Safe operating procedures: visual search, communication, distracted driving, speed and space management, night operation, extreme driving conditions.
  3. Advanced operating practices: hazard perception, skid control and recovery, jackknifing and other emergencies, railroad-highway grade crossings.
  4. Vehicle systems and reporting malfunctions: identifying and diagnosing malfunctions, roadside inspections, maintenance.
  5. Non-driving activities: handling and documenting cargo, environmental compliance, hours of service, fatigue and wellness, post-crash procedures, external communications, whistleblower and coercion rules, trip planning, drugs and alcohol, medical requirements.

Endorsement theory (H, P, S) covers topics specific to hazmat, passengers, or school buses. See the ELDT curriculum guide.

What behind-the-wheel training covers

Range training (Class A example): vehicle inspection, straight-line backing, alley dock backing, offset backing, parallel parking (sight side and blind side), and coupling and uncoupling.

Public-road training: vehicle controls in traffic (turns, lane changes, curves, entering and exiting highways), shifting, visual search, communication and signaling, speed and space management, safe driver behavior, hazard perception, railroad crossings, and night or adverse conditions where possible.

Class A and B require both range and public-road BTW, and both must come from the same BTW provider. For P and S endorsements, BTW is conducted on a range and/or public road in a passenger vehicle or school bus.

Which order should you do them in?

FMCSA does not require a specific order. Many drivers do theory first because:

  • It is online and can start immediately, even before you have a CLP
  • It teaches the inspection steps, terminology, and rules you'll practice in BTW
  • It often helps with the CLP knowledge tests

The only timing rule is that BTW must be finished before your skills test, and you need a commercial learner's permit to drive on public roads during BTW.

Can theory and BTW be from different providers?

Yes. Theory and BTW can be delivered by separate providers, as long as both are on the Training Provider Registry. Each provider reports its own portion. A common path: online theory from a provider like ELDT Training, then BTW at a local CDL school or with a carrier.

How BTW is evaluated

There is no hour count. Your BTW instructor must observe you performing every required skill proficiently. Drivers with prior farm, military, or construction equipment experience may finish quickly; brand-new drivers often need more practice. Ask a BTW provider how they price training: by the hour, by package, or by a flat fee to proficiency.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do behind-the-wheel training online?

No. BTW must take place in a real commercial vehicle with a qualified instructor. FMCSA does not allow driving simulators to replace BTW training.

Do I need BTW for the hazmat endorsement?

No. The H endorsement is theory only. Your state checks your hazmat theory record before the H knowledge test.

How many hours of behind-the-wheel training do I need?

The federal rule has no minimum. Your instructor decides when you are proficient. Some states and employers set their own hour requirements.

Does my CDL school count as both theory and BTW?

If the school is registered on the TPR for both theory and BTW in your training type, yes. Confirm both are listed for the school's location.

Can I do theory in one state and BTW in another?

Yes. Online theory is not tied to a state. In-person BTW must meet the rules of the state where it is delivered, and your home state may add requirements, so check before you book.
