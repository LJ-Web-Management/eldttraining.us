ELDT for Employers and Fleets: What You Need to Know

How fleets keep new-driver pipelines moving under ELDT, and where online theory fits.

Updated November 16, 20263 min readReviewed by the ELDT Training curriculum team
Quick answer

Employers don't take ELDT themselves, but every new hire getting a first-time Class A or B CDL, a B-to-A upgrade, or a first-time H, P, or S endorsement must complete ELDT with registered providers before testing. Many fleets outsource theory to an online provider and deliver behind-the-wheel training in-house after registering their own BTW program on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry. Either way, training must come from providers active on the registry.

Registering as a BTW provider

To deliver BTW in-house, you register each training location on the TPR and self-certify that you meet the requirements: qualified BTW instructors, representative vehicles in safe operating condition, range facilities that allow all required maneuvers, a curriculum covering every BTW topic, and compliance with any state requirements where training occurs. You must then report each driver's BTW completion by midnight of the second business day.

Why online theory makes sense for fleets

  • No classroom or instructor time for theory
  • New hires start immediately, even before their CLP
  • Consistent curriculum across locations
  • Low cost per driver: $129.99 Class A, $119.99 Class B, $59.99 to $69.99 endorsements here
  • Documented completion reported to the TPR

Compliance checklist for employers

  • Verify any outside provider is active on the TPR before sending drivers
  • Keep records of each driver's ELDT completions (certificate and provider confirmation)
  • Remember ELDT applies to endorsements too: a current driver adding first-time hazmat, passenger, or school bus needs ELDT for it
  • Watch for FMCSA notices of proposed removal if you're a registered provider, and fix deficiencies quickly
  • Make sure new hires meet the English language proficiency requirement

School districts and transit agencies

New school bus drivers typically need Class B, P, and S ELDT. Many districts register as BTW providers and buy online theory for all three. See school bus endorsement.

Frequently asked questions

Can employers buy ELDT courses for drivers?

Yes. Employers can purchase courses and provide logins to drivers. Each driver's completion is reported individually to the TPR under the driver's license information.

Does an existing driver need ELDT to add an endorsement?

Only for first-time H, P, or S endorsements (and B-to-A upgrades). N and T don't require ELDT.

Can our trainers deliver theory too?

Yes, if your organization registers as a theory provider and meets the theory instructor and curriculum requirements. Many fleets find outsourcing theory simpler.

What happens to our hires if our outside school is removed from the TPR?

Training delivered after the removal date is invalid. Verify providers before enrolling drivers and monitor their status.
