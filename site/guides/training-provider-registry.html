FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR): What It Is and How to Use It

The TPR is the federal list of approved ELDT trainers, and the database your state checks before testing. Here's how to use it to protect yourself.

Updated November 16, 20264 min readReviewed by the ELDT Training curriculum team
Quick answer

The FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR) at tpr.fmcsa.dot.gov is the official federal list of organizations allowed to deliver Entry-Level Driver Training, and the system that stores each driver's ELDT completion records. Only training from a provider listed on the TPR counts. Providers must report your completion by midnight of the second business day, and your state licensing agency checks the TPR before your CDL skills test or H knowledge test. Always search the registry before you pay for training.

What the TPR does

  • Lists registered providers. Schools, carriers, community colleges, unions, school districts, and online providers self-certify that they meet 49 CFR Part 380 requirements and receive a TPR identification number.
  • Stores driver training records. Providers submit each driver's completed theory and BTW training.
  • Feeds state licensing agencies. States query the TPR before administering the relevant test.
  • Lets FMCSA remove providers. FMCSA can propose removal of providers that don't meet the requirements and remove them from the registry.

How to check a provider on the TPR

  1. Go to tpr.fmcsa.dot.gov and choose the option to search for a training provider.
  2. Search by provider name, city, state, or ZIP code.
  3. Open the listing and confirm the training types offered (Class A, Class B, P, S, H) and whether it offers theory, BTW range, and/or BTW public road.
  4. For in-person training, confirm the location where you'll train is listed.
  5. Check that the provider is active (not removed or under a notice of proposed removal).

Only pay a provider that's active on the registry

Training delivered after a provider's removal date is invalid under federal rules. If a provider receives a notice of proposed removal, it must tell enrolled students.

TPR removals since 2025: what drivers should know

FMCSA has significantly stepped up enforcement of the registry. In December 2025, FMCSA announced the removal of more than 3,000 providers and sent notices of proposed removal to thousands more. In 2026 FMCSA continued removing providers, including emergency removals tied to compliance problems. Many removed listings were inactive, but the lesson for drivers is the same:

  • Verify before you enroll, not after.
  • Keep your receipts and certificate of completion.
  • Complete training promptly once you start.
  • If your provider is removed while you're mid-training, contact your state licensing agency before you test.

How the TPR handles your record

  1. You complete theory (80%+) or BTW.
  2. The provider submits your name, license number and state, training type, and completion date with its TPR ID.
  3. The record becomes available to state licensing agencies.
  4. Before your test, the state verifies the record. No record, no test.

Drivers can't submit or edit records; only the provider that trained you can correct a record it submitted. See ELDT certificate for troubleshooting.

Frequently asked questions

What is the TPR in trucking?

TPR stands for Training Provider Registry, FMCSA's database of approved Entry-Level Driver Training providers and the driver training records those providers submit.

Are "FMCSA approved" and "TPR registered" the same thing?

FMCSA does not individually approve or endorse schools. Providers self-certify and are listed on the TPR, and FMCSA can audit and remove them. "Listed on the TPR" is the accurate term.

How do I know if my ELDT was submitted to the TPR?

Ask your provider for the submission date. Providers must submit by midnight of the second business day after completion. Your state will confirm when you schedule or arrive for your test.

Can an employer use the TPR?

Employers can search the registry to verify providers. Carriers that train their own drivers can register as providers themselves. See ELDT for employers.

What happens to my record if my school closes?

Records already submitted remain in the registry. If your school closed before submitting, contact your state licensing agency.
