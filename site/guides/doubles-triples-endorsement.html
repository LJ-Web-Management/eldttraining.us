Doubles and Triples Endorsement (T): Requirements and Test

The T endorsement is a knowledge-test-only add-on for Class A drivers who pull more than one trailer.

Updated November 16, 20262 min readReviewed by the ELDT Training curriculum team
Quick answer

The T endorsement lets a Class A CDL holder pull double or triple trailers. You get it by passing your state's doubles/triples knowledge test; there's no skills test, and the federal ELDT rule doesn't apply to the T endorsement. Doubles are common in LTL freight; triples are only legal on certain highways in some states.

On this page
  1. T endorsement requirements
  2. What's on the doubles/triples test
  3. Where doubles and triples are used

T endorsement requirements

  • A Class A CDL (or Class A CLP while training)
  • Passing score on the doubles/triples knowledge test (commonly about 20 questions, 80% to pass)
  • State endorsement fee

No ELDT and no skills test are federally required, though employers that run doubles usually provide on-the-job training.

What's on the doubles/triples test

  • Coupling and uncoupling a second or third trailer, including converter dollies
  • Inspecting the extra connections: air lines, electrical lines, safety chains, pintle hooks
  • Checking air flow to the last trailer
  • Crack-the-whip effect and rearward amplification
  • Rollover risk and the importance of a light trailer in the rear
  • Managing space, following distance, and turning with multiple trailers

Where doubles and triples are used

Double "pup" trailers (about 28 feet each) are a staple of less-than-truckload (LTL) carriers. Triples are permitted on certain routes, mostly in western and some midwestern states, and on specific toll roads. Rules on lengths and routes vary by state.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need ELDT for a doubles/triples endorsement?

No. Federal ELDT covers Class A, Class B, H, P, and S only. Your Class A ELDT already covers combination-vehicle fundamentals.

Can I get a T endorsement with a Class B CDL?

No. Pulling doubles or triples requires a Class A CDL.

Is the T endorsement worth getting?

If you want LTL work, yes. It's inexpensive, quick to add, and many LTL carriers require it.

Can I add T to my CLP?

Only P, S, and N can be placed on a CLP, so T is added once you hold your Class A CDL. You can usually take the knowledge test with your other permit tests.
