ELDT Training Requirements: Who Needs It and What's Required

Every requirement in the federal ELDT rule, translated into plain English, with the regulation behind each one.

Updated November 16, 20265 min readReviewed by the ELDT Training curriculum team
Quick answer

Under the federal ELDT rule (49 CFR Part 380), you must complete entry-level driver training from an FMCSA-registered provider before testing if you are getting a first-time Class A or Class B CDL, upgrading from Class B to Class A, or getting a first-time H, P, or S endorsement. Theory training requires an 80% score on the assessment. Class A, Class B, P, and S also require behind-the-wheel training. There are no minimum hours, and your provider must report completion to the Training Provider Registry before your state will test you.

Requirement 1: Who must complete ELDT

The trigger is the license or endorsement you are applying for, not your job or employer. Under 49 CFR 380.609, ELDT is required for an individual who:

  • Applies for a Class A or Class B CDL for the first time
  • Applies to upgrade a Class B CDL to a Class A CDL
  • Applies for a Hazardous Materials (H), Passenger (P), or School Bus (S) endorsement for the first time

Not covered: the Tanker (N) endorsement, the Doubles/Triples (T) endorsement, and removing restrictions such as the air brake (L/Z) or manual transmission (E) restriction. A first-time Class C CDL by itself does not trigger ELDT, but adding a P, S, or H endorsement to a Class C does. See ELDT for Class C.

Requirement 2: Train with a registered provider

All ELDT must be delivered by a provider listed on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR). Providers self-certify that they meet the curriculum, instructor, facility, and vehicle requirements, and FMCSA can remove providers that do not comply. Training received from a provider after it has been removed from the registry is not valid.

You may use different providers for theory and behind-the-wheel. Both must be registered, and each reports its own portion.

Requirement 3: Complete the theory curriculum and score 80%

Each training type has its own curriculum in the appendices to Part 380:

Hazardous Materials (H) endorsementAppendix E

The provider must cover every topic in the curriculum. You must score at least 80% overall on the written or electronic theory assessment. There is no minimum number of classroom hours. Online delivery is allowed. Learn what's covered in our ELDT curriculum guide.

Requirement 4: Complete behind-the-wheel training (A, B, P, S)

Behind-the-wheel (BTW) training must take place in a representative vehicle for the license class or endorsement, with a qualified BTW instructor.

  • Class A and Class B: both range training (for example, backing, docking, and coupling for Class A) and public-road training.
  • P and S endorsements: range and/or public-road training specific to passenger vehicles and school buses.
  • H endorsement: no BTW requirement.

BTW has no minimum hours. Completion is based on the instructor's assessment of your proficiency in every required skill. FMCSA does not allow driving simulators to substitute for BTW training. Both portions of BTW (range and public road) must be delivered by the same provider.

Requirement 5: Qualified instructors

ELDT instructors generally must hold a CDL of the same or higher class with any needed endorsements and have at least two years of experience driving a CMV requiring that CDL, or at least two years as a BTW instructor. Online theory content must be prepared and delivered by instructors who meet the federal definition of a theory instructor. States may add their own instructor licensing rules for in-person training.

Requirement 6: Reporting to the TPR

After you complete a portion of training, the provider must electronically submit your training certification to the TPR by midnight of the second business day. The record includes your name, driver's license number and state, the training type, completion date, and the provider's TPR number.

Requirement 7: State verification before testing

Your state driver licensing agency (SDLA) must confirm the TPR record before it administers:

  • The Class A or Class B skills test
  • The P or S skills test
  • The H knowledge test

The state may schedule your test in advance, but it cannot give the test until the training record is available.

What ELDT does not require

  • No minimum hours for theory or BTW.
  • No required order. You can do theory and BTW in any order, though BTW must be finished before the skills test.
  • No CLP before theory. You can start theory before you have a commercial learner's permit.
  • No ELDT for N or T endorsements, or for restriction removals.

Frequently asked questions

Are ELDT requirements the same in every state?

The federal requirements are the same everywhere and every state uses the same Training Provider Registry. States may add requirements, especially for in-person BTW training delivered within their borders, so check with your state licensing agency.

Does ELDT require 160 hours of training?

No. Some CDL schools advertise 160-hour programs, but the federal ELDT rule has no minimum hours. Training is complete when you pass the theory assessment with 80% or more and your BTW instructor certifies proficiency.

Do I need ELDT for a learner's permit?

No. ELDT is not required to get a CLP. It is required before the skills test (Class A, B, P, S) or the H knowledge test.

Do drivers with military experience need ELDT?

Military personnel who qualify for the skills-test waiver under 49 CFR 383.77 are not subject to ELDT. Check with your state's military CDL waiver program.

Is ELDT required for an intrastate-only CDL?

Yes. ELDT applies to Class A and Class B CDLs regardless of whether they carry an intrastate (K) restriction.
