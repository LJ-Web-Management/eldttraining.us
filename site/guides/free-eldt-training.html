Free ELDT Training: Real Ways to Get ELDT Paid For

Free study guides are easy to find. Free training that actually counts toward your CDL is rarer. Here's where to look, and what to avoid.

Updated November 16, 20263 min readReviewed by the ELDT Training curriculum team
Quick answer

ELDT only counts if it comes from a provider on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry, and registered providers almost always charge for it. "Free" ELDT usually means someone else pays: an employer, a carrier with a company-sponsored CDL program, a school district or transit agency, a WIOA workforce grant, or veterans education benefits. Free practice tests and study guides are useful for learning, but they do not satisfy the ELDT requirement.

Use free materials to study for your CDL permit test, but plan on a registered provider for the training itself.

Legitimate ways to get ELDT paid for

1. Your employer

Many employers reimburse or directly purchase ELDT for new hires: construction companies, municipalities, utilities, waste haulers, beverage distributors, and fuel companies. Ask HR before you enroll. Employers can buy courses for drivers and give you the login.

2. Company-sponsored CDL training

Some large carriers train new drivers at no upfront cost in exchange for a work commitment (often around a year). If you leave early, you may owe some or all of the training cost. Read the contract carefully. See paid CDL training.

3. School districts and transit agencies

School bus contractors, districts, and public transit agencies often pay for the Class B, P, and S training their drivers need, and may pay you while you train.

4. WIOA workforce funding

The Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act funds job training through local American Job Centers. CDL training is a common approved program. Funding usually goes to providers on your state's eligible training provider list, so ask your local center which programs qualify.

5. Veterans benefits

Veterans may be able to use GI Bill benefits at approved CDL programs. Many veterans qualify for the military skills test waiver, which also exempts them from ELDT for that license class. See ELDT exemptions.

6. Low-cost online theory

If no one is paying, online theory is the cheapest legitimate option. Our hazmat course is $69.99 and Class A is $129.99. Doing theory online can also cut what you pay a CDL school for BTW.

Red flags with "free ELDT" offers

  • The provider isn't on the Training Provider Registry
  • They only offer a "certificate" with no final assessment
  • They ask for personal information or payment for a "TPR filing fee" separate from training
  • The training contract has steep repayment penalties you don't understand

Frequently asked questions

Is there free hazmat ELDT training online?

Not usually from registered providers. Your employer may pay. Our hazmat ELDT course is $69.99 and is theory only, so it's the whole ELDT requirement for the H endorsement.

Do trucking companies pay for ELDT?

Many do, either through company-sponsored CDL schools or by reimbursing new hires. Terms vary, so read any training agreement carefully.

Can I use free practice tests instead of ELDT?

No. Practice tests can help you pass state knowledge tests, but your state requires a TPR record from a registered provider before the skills test.

Will my state pay for CDL training?

Some states fund CDL training through workforce grants and community colleges. Contact your local American Job Center.
