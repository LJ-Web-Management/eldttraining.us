School Bus Endorsement (S): How to Get It

To drive a school bus you need a CDL with two endorsements, two ELDT courses, and usually some state-specific training. Here's the full path.

Updated November 16, 20263 min readReviewed by the ELDT Training curriculum team
Quick answer

To drive a school bus you need a CDL (usually Class B, sometimes C) with both the Passenger (P) and School Bus (S) endorsements. First-time applicants must complete ELDT for each endorsement (theory and behind-the-wheel) with FMCSA-registered providers, plus Class B ELDT if it's your first Class B CDL. Then you pass the school bus and passenger knowledge tests and a skills test in a school bus. Most states also require background checks and their own school bus driver training.

What S endorsement ELDT covers

The school bus curriculum (Appendix D to Part 380) focuses on danger zones and mirror use, loading and unloading, student management, emergency exit and evacuation, railroad-highway grade crossings, antilock braking systems, and special safety considerations. Our S course is 11 lessons, about 5 hours, for $59.99.

Step-by-step path to becoming a school bus driver

  1. Apply with a school district or contractor (many pay for training and testing).
  2. DOT physical and CLP with P and S knowledge tests (S and P can appear on a CLP; no passengers allowed while on a CLP).
  3. Complete Class B, P, and S ELDT theory online.
  4. Complete BTW in a school bus with a registered provider (often the district itself).
  5. Pass the skills test in a school bus.
  6. Complete your state's school bus certification requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Do school bus drivers need both P and S?

Yes. Federal rules require the P endorsement for vehicles designed for 16+ occupants, and the S endorsement specifically for school buses.

Does my school district pay for training?

Many districts and contractors pay for CDL training, ELDT, and testing, and some pay you while you train. Ask during hiring.

Can I drive a school bus with a Class C CDL?

For smaller school buses under 26,001 lbs GVWR, yes, with P and S endorsements. Full-size buses require Class B.

Is the school bus endorsement test hard?

The knowledge test is typically about 20 questions. The skills test includes school-bus-specific inspection items and procedures like loading and unloading and railroad crossings, so practice them during BTW.
