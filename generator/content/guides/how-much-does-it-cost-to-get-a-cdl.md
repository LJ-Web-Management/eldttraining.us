:::answer
**Getting a CDL typically costs about $3,500 to $10,000 at a private CDL school, mostly for training.** You can spend much less by doing ELDT theory online ($119.99 to $129.99 here for Class A or B) and buying behind-the-wheel training separately, or almost nothing upfront with employer-paid or company-sponsored training. Non-training costs (DOT physical, permit, skills test, license) usually add a few hundred dollars depending on your state.
:::

## CDL cost breakdown

| Cost | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ELDT theory | $50 to $150 online | Class A $129.99, Class B $119.99 at ELDT Training |
| Behind-the-wheel training | ~$1,500 to $5,000+ | Hourly or packaged; varies widely |
| Full CDL school (theory + BTW) | ~$3,000 to $10,000 | Often includes test-day truck |
| DOT physical | ~$75 to $150 | National Registry examiner |
| CLP fee | Varies by state | Often includes knowledge tests |
| Skills test fee | Varies by state | Some states use paid third-party testers |
| CDL license fee | Varies by state | Plus endorsement fees |
| Truck for skills test | $0 to several hundred | Usually included with BTW programs |
| TSA threat assessment (H/X) | Under $100 | Paid to TSA's enrollment provider |

State fees vary a lot. Check your state licensing agency's fee schedule; our [state pages](/pages/state) link to each agency.

## Three ways to pay for a CDL

### 1. Private CDL school: highest cost, most convenience
One provider handles theory, BTW, and often the test truck. Expect roughly $3,000 to $10,000 for Class A.

### 2. Online ELDT theory + separate BTW: often the best value
Finish federally required theory online at your own pace, then buy only the BTW training you need from a registered provider. This often cuts total cost and lets you keep working while you study.

### 3. Employer-paid or company-sponsored training: lowest upfront cost
Carriers, school districts, transit agencies, and municipalities may pay for training, sometimes with a work commitment. Read repayment terms carefully. See [paid CDL training](/guides/paid-cdl-training).

## Hidden costs to budget for

- Retest fees if you fail a knowledge or skills test
- Travel to a test site and time off work
- Medical specialist visits if your DOT exam needs extra paperwork
- Renewals: medical certificate (up to every 24 months) and hazmat threat assessment (every 5 years)

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the cheapest way to get a CDL?
Employer-paid training is cheapest upfront. If you're paying yourself, online ELDT theory plus BTW-only training is usually cheaper than an all-inclusive school.

### Is a Class B CDL cheaper than Class A?
Usually. Class B BTW is shorter and Class B theory is a bit cheaper ($119.99 vs $129.99 here). But Class A qualifies you for more jobs and also lets you take the Class B skills test.

### Can I use financial aid for a CDL?
Some community college CDL programs accept federal financial aid, and WIOA workforce funding or GI Bill benefits may cover approved programs.

### How much do CDL endorsements cost?
Expect state knowledge-test and endorsement fees, plus ELDT for H, P, or S ($59.99 to $69.99 online here), and the TSA assessment for hazmat.
