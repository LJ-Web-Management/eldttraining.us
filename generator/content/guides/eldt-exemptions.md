:::answer
**You don't need ELDT if you already held the CDL class or endorsement before February 7, 2022, if you got your CLP before that date and obtain your CDL before the CLP (or renewed CLP) expires, or if you are excepted from the CDL skills test**, such as qualifying military drivers using the skills-test waiver under 49 CFR 383.77. ELDT also isn't required for the Tanker (N) or Doubles/Triples (T) endorsements or for removing L, Z, or E restrictions.
:::

## Exemption 1: You held the license or endorsement before February 7, 2022

ELDT is not retroactive. If you held a valid Class A or Class B CDL, or an H, P, or S endorsement, issued before February 7, 2022, you don't need ELDT for that same license class or endorsement.

**Lapsed licenses:** Under 49 CFR 380.603, drivers who previously held a CDL issued before February 7, 2022 and are re-obtaining a CDL of the same class are not subject to ELDT, even if the CDL lapsed. Your state may still require you to retake tests.

## Exemption 2: You had a CLP before February 7, 2022

If you obtained a commercial learner's permit before February 7, 2022, you are not subject to ELDT **as long as you obtain your CDL before that CLP or renewed CLP expires**. If your permit expired and you have to get a new one, ELDT applies. Since CLPs last at most about a year, this exemption has largely run out.

## Exemption 3: You're excepted from the CDL skills test

The ELDT rule didn't create new exceptions; it follows the existing CDL skills-test exceptions. You're not subject to ELDT if you're applying under:

- **Military skills-test waiver** (49 CFR 383.77) for current or recently separated service members who operated equivalent military vehicles
- Exceptions in 49 CFR 383.3(c), (d), and (h), such as certain farm, firefighter, and military-vehicle operator exceptions where the state doesn't require a CDL
- **Restricted CDLs** under 49 CFR 383.3(e) through (g) for certain farm-related service industries and similar seasonal operations, where states issue them

## Exemption 4: Restriction removals

Removing these restrictions does not require ELDT:

- **L or Z** (air brake restrictions)
- **E** (no manual transmission)

For restriction removal under 49 CFR 383.135(b)(7), check your state's process. Most require a skills test in the right vehicle. See [CDL restrictions](/guides/cdl-restrictions).

## Not exempt: endorsements and upgrades people often miss

| Situation | ELDT needed? |
|---|---|
| Class B holder upgrading to Class A | **Yes**, Class A training |
| Class A holder adding first-time hazmat | **Yes**, H theory |
| CDL holder adding first-time P or S | **Yes**, P or S theory + BTW |
| Adding Tanker (N) | No (federal) |
| Adding Doubles/Triples (T) | No |
| Class C holder adding first-time H, P, or S | **Yes**, for that endorsement |
| Class A holder who wants to drive Class B vehicles | No |

## Military drivers: ELDT and the skills test waiver

Service members and veterans who qualify for their state's military skills test waiver are excepted from the skills test, and therefore from ELDT, for that license class. If you don't qualify for the waiver (for example, too much time has passed since separation), ELDT applies like any other new driver. Many states also offer military knowledge-test waivers; check your state's program.

## Frequently asked questions

### Am I grandfathered from ELDT?
If you held the same CDL class or endorsement before February 7, 2022, yes. Getting a new class or endorsement for the first time (for example, upgrading B to A or adding hazmat) still requires ELDT for that new class or endorsement.

### My CDL expired years ago. Do I need ELDT?
Under 49 CFR 380.603, if your earlier CDL was issued before February 7, 2022 and you're re-obtaining the same class, ELDT doesn't apply. Your state may still require you to retest.

### Do farmers need ELDT?
Farmers who qualify for a state's farm exception from the CDL aren't getting a CDL, so ELDT doesn't apply. If a farmer gets a regular Class A or B CDL, ELDT applies.

### Do I need ELDT to transfer my CDL to a new state?
No. Transferring an existing CDL of the same class to a new state of residence is not a first-time CDL.
