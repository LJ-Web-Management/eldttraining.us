**ELDT "certification" is an electronic record your training provider submits to the FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR), not a paper card you hand to the DMV.** After you pass the theory assessment with 80% or higher (or complete BTW), the provider must submit your certification by midnight of the second business day. Your state licensing agency pulls that record before your skills test or H knowledge test. Many providers, including ours, also give you a downloadable certificate of completion for your own records.
:::

## What gets reported to the TPR

Under 49 CFR 380.717, the provider's electronic certification includes:

- Your full name, driver's license number, and state of licensure
- The training type completed (Class A, Class B, P, S, or H; theory or BTW)
- The date you completed the training
- For theory, confirmation that you passed the assessment (minimum 80%)
- The provider's name and TPR identification number

The record then becomes available to your state driver licensing agency (SDLA) when it checks before testing.

## Paper certificate vs TPR record

| | Certificate of completion | TPR certification record |
|---|---|---|
| Who creates it | Your training provider | Your training provider, submitted to FMCSA |
| Format | PDF you can download or print | Electronic record in the federal registry |
| Who uses it | You and your employer | Your state licensing agency |
| Required to test? | No | Yes |

Keep your certificate as proof for employers and in case you need to follow up, but remember the state relies on the electronic record.

## Why your information must match your license

If the name or license number you entered doesn't match your driver's license or CLP exactly, the state may not find your record. When you enroll:

- Use your full legal name as printed on your license or CLP
- Double-check your license number and issuing state
- If you get a CLP after starting theory, make sure your provider has the license number your state will look up

## How to check that your ELDT was submitted

Only registered providers can submit or view driver records in the TPR; drivers cannot enter or edit records themselves. To confirm:

1. **Ask your provider** for confirmation of the submission date.
2. **Wait at least two business days** after completion.
3. **Ask your state licensing agency** when you schedule your test whether your ELDT record is visible.

## What to do if your state can't find your ELDT

1. Confirm with your provider that the record was submitted and that the name and license number are correct. Providers can correct errors in records they submitted.
2. Confirm the training type matches the test (for example, Class A theory for a Class A skills test).
3. For Class A, B, P, and S, confirm **both** theory and BTW have been reported.
4. If the provider was removed from the registry, contact your state licensing agency right away about your options.

## Does an ELDT certificate expire?

FMCSA's Training Provider Registry FAQs do not set an expiration date for ELDT certifications. Your commercial learner's permit does expire, so schedule training and testing within its validity. See [how long ELDT results are valid](/guides/how-long-is-eldt-training).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to bring my ELDT certificate to the DMV?
Usually not. Your state checks the electronic record in the Training Provider Registry. Bring your certificate anyway in case the clerk asks, but the TPR record is what qualifies you to test.

### How do I get my ELDT certificate?
At ELDT Training you can download your certificate of completion right after you pass the final assessment. We also submit your completion to the TPR.

### Can I see my ELDT record in the TPR myself?
The registry's driver records are accessed by training providers and state licensing agencies. Drivers can search the public registry for providers but can't edit training records.

### Is ELDT certification the same as a CDL?
No. ELDT certification qualifies you to take the CDL skills test (or H knowledge test). You still have to pass the state test to get your CDL or endorsement.
