:::answer
**CDL restrictions are codes that limit which vehicles you can drive.** The most common are L (no air brakes), Z (no full air brakes), E (no manual transmission), O (no tractor-trailer), M and N (passenger vehicle class limits), K (intrastate only), and V (medical variance). Most restrictions come from the vehicle you use on the skills test. Removing L, Z, or E restrictions doesn't require ELDT; you typically just test in the right vehicle.
:::

## Common CDL restriction codes

| Code | Meaning | How you get it |
|---|---|---|
| **L** | No air brake equipped CMV | Failing or skipping the Air Brakes knowledge test, or testing in a vehicle without air brakes |
| **Z** | No full air brake equipped CMV | Testing in a vehicle with air-over-hydraulic brakes |
| **E** | No manual transmission CMV | Taking the skills test in an automatic |
| **O** | No tractor-trailer CMV | Class A test in a combination without a fifth-wheel connection (e.g., truck and pintle-hook trailer) |
| **M** | No Class A passenger vehicle | Class A holder with P/S endorsement tested in a Class B passenger vehicle |
| **N** | No Class A or B passenger vehicle | Class B holder with P/S endorsement tested in a Class C passenger vehicle |
| **K** | Intrastate only | Under 21, or doesn't meet interstate medical requirements |
| **V** | Medical variance | Driving under an FMCSA medical exemption or skill performance evaluation |
| **P** | No passengers in CMV bus | CLP holders with a P endorsement |
| **X** | No cargo in CMV tank vehicle | CLP holders with an N endorsement |

Note: the **N restriction** (passenger vehicle limit) is different from the **N endorsement** (tank vehicles).

## How to avoid restrictions

- Pass the **Air Brakes** knowledge test and test in a vehicle with full air brakes to avoid L and Z.
- Test in a **manual transmission** if you might drive one; many carriers now run automatics, so E may not matter for your job.
- For Class A, test in a **tractor-trailer** to avoid O.

## How to remove a restriction

Removing L, Z, or E typically means passing the relevant knowledge test (for L) and/or a skills test in a vehicle with the right equipment. The federal ELDT rule doesn't apply to removing these restrictions. K comes off when you turn 21 and meet interstate requirements, usually through a visit to your state agency.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an automatic transmission restriction matter?
It depends on your job. Many fleets now run automated manual transmissions, but some jobs (construction, older equipment) still require a manual. An E restriction rules those out.

### Do I need ELDT to remove the air brake restriction?
No. Removing L or Z restrictions isn't covered by ELDT. Check your state's process; it usually involves the Air Brakes knowledge test and a skills test in an air-brake vehicle.

### What's the difference between a restriction and an endorsement?
Endorsements add privileges (hazmat, tanker, passenger). Restrictions take privileges away. See [CDL endorsements](/guides/cdl-endorsements).

### Can I get a Class A without air brakes?
Yes, with an L restriction, but nearly all tractor-trailers have air brakes, so it severely limits your options.
