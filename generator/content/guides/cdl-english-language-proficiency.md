:::answer
**Federal rules (49 CFR 391.11(b)(2)) require commercial drivers to read and speak English well enough to converse with the public, understand highway traffic signs and signals, respond to official inquiries, and make entries on reports and records.** Since June 25, 2025, inspectors have placed drivers who fail this standard at roadside out of service. FMCSA has also cited English-proficiency problems when removing training providers from the Training Provider Registry in 2026.
:::

## What the rule requires

A driver must be able to:

- Read and speak English sufficiently to converse with the general public
- Understand highway traffic signs and signals in English
- Respond to official inquiries (for example, from law enforcement or inspectors)
- Make entries on reports and records (logs, inspection reports, shipping papers)

## What changed in 2025

The English proficiency requirement isn't new, but for several years a violation by itself didn't take a driver off the road. Following a 2025 executive order, the out-of-service criteria were updated so that, starting **June 25, 2025**, a driver found not proficient during a roadside inspection is placed **out of service** and can't continue driving until the issue is resolved.

## How it's checked

Inspectors typically assess proficiency by talking with the driver in English during the inspection and checking whether the driver can read and explain road signs. Interpreters and translation apps aren't a substitute during this assessment.

## What this means for new drivers

- **Training:** Many ELDT courses, including ours, are delivered in English. This also helps you practice the vocabulary used on the job.
- **Testing:** States administer CDL knowledge and skills tests under their own policies, but the on-the-job standard is English.
- **Providers:** In 2026, FMCSA announced emergency removals of training providers after finding English proficiency violations among their graduates. Check that your provider is active on the [Training Provider Registry](/guides/training-provider-registry).

## How to prepare

- Learn the English terms for every pre-trip inspection item and vehicle control
- Practice explaining what road signs mean, out loud
- Practice common conversations: with dispatch, shippers, receivers, and officers
- Use audio playback in your ELDT course to hear terms pronounced

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I take the CDL test in another language?
Some states offer knowledge tests in other languages. That doesn't change the federal on-the-job requirement that drivers read and speak English sufficiently.

### Is ELDT Training available in Spanish?
No. Our courses are English only, with audio playback that reads each lesson aloud.

### What happens if I'm placed out of service for English proficiency?
You can't continue operating the CMV at that time; another qualified driver must move the vehicle. Your employer will need to address the issue before you return to driving.

### Does this apply to intrastate drivers?
Federal driver qualification rules apply to interstate drivers, and most states adopt similar rules for intrastate commerce. Check your state's requirements.
