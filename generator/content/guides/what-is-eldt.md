**ELDT (Entry-Level Driver Training) is a Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) rule that sets the minimum training new commercial drivers must complete before they can take certain CDL tests.** It applies to anyone getting a Class A or Class B CDL for the first time, upgrading from Class B to Class A, or getting a Hazmat (H), Passenger (P), or School Bus (S) endorsement for the first time. Training must come from a provider listed on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR), and it has two parts: theory (which can be done online) and behind-the-wheel training.
:::

## What does ELDT stand for?

ELDT stands for **Entry-Level Driver Training**. You will see it written as "ELDT," "the ELDT rule," or "the ELDT mandate." It refers to the training requirements in 49 CFR Part 380, Subpart F, and the registry of approved trainers in Subpart G.

Before ELDT, there was no national training standard for new CDL drivers. Someone could study the state CDL manual, pass the knowledge test, practice with a friend, and take the skills test. FMCSA created ELDT to make sure every new driver receives the same core knowledge and supervised driving practice from a qualified trainer first.

## Who needs ELDT?

You must complete ELDT before testing if you are:

- Getting a **Class A CDL** for the first time
- Getting a **Class B CDL** for the first time
- **Upgrading** an existing Class B CDL to a Class A CDL
- Getting a **Passenger (P)** endorsement for the first time
- Getting a **School Bus (S)** endorsement for the first time
- Getting a **Hazardous Materials (H)** endorsement for the first time

ELDT is not retroactive. If you held the CDL or endorsement before February 7, 2022, you do not need ELDT for it. ELDT also does not apply to the Tanker (N) or Doubles/Triples (T) endorsements. ## The two parts of ELDT: theory and behind-the-wheel

**Theory training** is the knowledge portion. It covers federal regulations, vehicle systems, safe operating practices, hours of service, cargo, trip planning, drugs and alcohol, and more. Theory can be delivered online. You must score at least **80%** on the theory assessment.

**Behind-the-wheel (BTW) training** happens in a real commercial vehicle with a qualified instructor. For Class A and Class B it includes both range training (backing, docking, coupling) and public-road training. For P and S endorsements it includes range and/or public-road training specific to buses. The Hazmat (H) endorsement has no BTW requirement.

There is no minimum number of hours for either part. Theory is complete when you pass the assessment; BTW is complete when your instructor determines you are proficient in every required skill. Learn more in [ELDT theory vs behind-the-wheel training](/guides/eldt-theory-vs-behind-the-wheel).

## How ELDT works, step by step

1. **Choose a registered provider.** Every provider must be listed on the [FMCSA Training Provider Registry](/guides/training-provider-registry). You can take theory and BTW from different providers.
2. **Complete theory training.** Online theory lets you study on your schedule. You can start before or after you get your [commercial learner's permit](/guides/cdl-permit-clp).
3. **Complete BTW training** (Class A, B, P, and S only) with a registered BTW provider such as a CDL school or a carrier's training program.
4. **Your provider reports your completion to the TPR** by midnight of the second business day after you finish.
5. **Take your test.** Your state driver licensing agency checks the TPR before it lets you take the CDL skills test (or the H knowledge test).

## Why FMCSA created the ELDT rule

Congress directed FMCSA to set entry-level training standards in the 2012 MAP-21 highway law. FMCSA published the final ELDT rule in December 2016 after a negotiated rulemaking with trucking, bus, safety, and training groups, and set the compliance date as February 7, 2022. The goal is safer roads: drivers who have learned the same core curriculum and shown proficiency to a qualified instructor before they test.

## How much does ELDT cost?

Online theory courses typically cost far less than a full CDL school. At ELDT Training, Class A theory is $129.99, Class B is $119.99, and endorsements start at $49.99. BTW training is usually the larger cost because it requires a truck, a range, and an instructor. See our [ELDT cost guide](/guides/eldt-cost) for a full breakdown.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the meaning of ELDT for CDL?
For CDL applicants, ELDT means the Entry-Level Driver Training you must complete with an FMCSA-registered provider before your state lets you take the Class A or B skills test, the P or S skills test, or the H knowledge test.

### Is ELDT the same as CDL school?
Not exactly. ELDT is the federal minimum training standard. A CDL school is one type of provider that may deliver both parts. You can also complete theory online with one provider and BTW with another, as long as both are on the Training Provider Registry.

### Do I need ELDT if I already have a CDL?
Only if you are upgrading from Class B to Class A or adding a first-time H, P, or S endorsement. Drivers who held their CDL before February 7, 2022 do not need ELDT for that same license class.

### Can ELDT be done online?
The theory portion can be completed entirely online. Behind-the-wheel training cannot; it must be done in a vehicle with a qualified instructor. FMCSA does not allow simulators to replace BTW training.

### What happens if I don't complete ELDT?
Your state licensing agency will not let you take the skills test for Class A, Class B, P, or S, or the knowledge test for H, until a registered provider has reported your training to the TPR.

### Does ELDT expire?
FMCSA's Training Provider Registry FAQs do not set an expiration date for ELDT certification. What does expire is your commercial learner's permit, so plan your training and testing within your CLP's validity. See [how long ELDT training takes](/guides/how-long-is-eldt-training).
