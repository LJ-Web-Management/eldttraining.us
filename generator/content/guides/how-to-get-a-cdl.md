:::answer
**To get a CDL: (1) choose your license class and endorsements, (2) pass a DOT physical, (3) pass your state's CDL knowledge tests to get a commercial learner's permit (CLP), (4) hold the CLP at least 14 days, (5) complete ELDT theory and behind-the-wheel training with FMCSA-registered providers, and (6) pass the CDL skills test.** You must be at least 18 (21 to drive across state lines). With focused effort, many people finish in 4 to 8 weeks.
:::

:::facts
Minimum age: 18 intrastate / 21 interstate
CLP holding period: 14 days minimum
ELDT required: Yes, for first-time Class A and B
Skills test parts: Inspection, basic control, road
Typical timeline: 4 to 8 weeks
:::

## Step 1: Choose your CDL class and endorsements

- **Class A:** tractor-trailers and other combinations over 26,000 lbs towing more than 10,000 lbs. The most flexible license. See [Class A CDL](/guides/class-a-cdl).
- **Class B:** heavy single vehicles such as dump trucks, box trucks, and buses. See [Class B CDL](/guides/class-b-cdl).
- **Class C:** smaller vehicles carrying 16+ passengers or placarded hazmat. See [ELDT for Class C](/guides/eldt-class-c).

Then decide on [endorsements](/guides/cdl-endorsements): Hazmat (H), Tanker (N), Passenger (P), School Bus (S), Doubles/Triples (T), or X. Not sure? Read [Class A vs Class B](/guides/class-a-vs-class-b-cdl).

## Step 2: Meet the basic requirements

You'll need a valid non-commercial driver's license, proof of identity, U.S. citizenship or lawful presence, state residency, and a Social Security number. You must be able to read and speak English well enough to do the job. Your state will also check your driving record and the FMCSA Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse. Full list: [CDL requirements](/guides/cdl-requirements).

## Step 3: Pass a DOT physical

Get a DOT physical from a medical examiner listed on the FMCSA National Registry. If you pass, you'll receive a medical examiner's certificate, valid for up to 24 months. See [DOT physical and medical card](/guides/dot-physical-medical-card).

## Step 4: Get your commercial learner's permit (CLP)

Study your state's CDL manual and pass the knowledge tests at your state licensing agency:

- **General Knowledge** (everyone)
- **Air Brakes** (if you'll drive air-brake vehicles, which most trucks have)
- **Combination Vehicles** (Class A)
- Endorsement tests you need (P, S, N, H, T)

You must hold your CLP for **at least 14 days** before taking the skills test. A CLP is valid for up to one year depending on your state. See [CDL permit (CLP) guide](/guides/cdl-permit-clp) and [CDL permit test](/guides/cdl-permit-test).

## Step 5: Complete ELDT theory training

If you're getting a Class A or B CDL for the first time (or upgrading B to A), you must complete ELDT theory with a provider on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry and score 80% or higher. Theory can be done online, before or after you get your CLP. Our [Class A](/products/entry-level-driver-training-eldt-class-a) and [Class B](/products/entry-level-driver-training-eldt-class-b) courses are self-paced and reported to the TPR when you pass.

## Step 6: Complete behind-the-wheel training

Train in a real truck with a TPR-registered BTW provider (a CDL school, community college, or carrier program), on a range and on public roads. There's no minimum hours; your instructor certifies when you're proficient. See [theory vs behind-the-wheel](/guides/eldt-theory-vs-behind-the-wheel).

## Step 7: Pass the CDL skills test

Your state checks the TPR for your ELDT records, then you take the three-part skills test in a vehicle representative of your license class:

1. **Vehicle inspection** (pre-trip)
2. **Basic vehicle control** (backing maneuvers)
3. **On-road driving test**

Prepare with our [pre-trip inspection checklist](/guides/pre-trip-inspection-checklist) and [CDL skills test guide](/guides/cdl-skills-test).

## Step 8: Get your CDL and start driving

Pass the skills test, pay your state fee, and receive your CDL. Keep your medical certificate current, and remember that adding a first-time H, P, or S endorsement later requires ELDT for that endorsement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get a CDL without going to CDL school?
Yes, as long as you complete ELDT theory and BTW with registered providers. Many drivers take theory online and BTW from an employer or a BTW-only provider. See [how to get a CDL without school](/guides/how-to-get-a-cdl-without-school).

### How hard is it to get a CDL?
It takes real study and practice, but most people who prepare pass. The parts that trip people up most are the air brakes knowledge test, the pre-trip inspection, and backing maneuvers.

### How long does it take to get a CDL?
Typically 4 to 8 weeks with focused effort, with a 14-day minimum between getting your CLP and taking the skills test. See [how long it takes to get a CDL](/guides/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-a-cdl).

### How much does it cost to get a CDL?
Anywhere from a few hundred dollars (if an employer covers BTW) to $10,000+ at private schools. See [CDL cost](/guides/how-much-does-it-cost-to-get-a-cdl).

### Can I get a CDL in a different state than I live in?
You generally must get your CDL from your state of domicile. You can train elsewhere, and some states let you take the skills test out of state and have results transferred.
