:::answer
**A DOT physical is a medical exam by a certified medical examiner on the FMCSA National Registry that checks whether you can safely drive a commercial vehicle.** It covers vision (at least 20/40 in each eye, corrected or not), hearing, blood pressure, a urinalysis, and a review of your health history. If you pass, you get a medical examiner's certificate valid for up to 24 months (less if you have a condition that needs monitoring). Exams commonly cost about $75 to $150.
:::

## What the DOT physical checks

| Area | Standard (summary) |
|---|---|
| Vision | At least 20/40 in each eye and both eyes together, with or without correction; 70° field of vision in each eye; recognize red, green, amber |
| Hearing | Perceive a forced whisper at 5 feet, or pass an audiometric test |
| Blood pressure | Below 140/90 for a full 2-year card; higher readings may mean a shorter card or disqualification until controlled |
| Urinalysis | Screens for protein, blood, and sugar (not a drug test) |
| Health history | Heart, lung, neurological, diabetes, sleep disorders, medications |

A **drug test** is separate. Employers require a pre-employment DOT drug test for CDL jobs.

## Conditions that need extra steps

- **Insulin-treated diabetes:** allowed with a treating clinician's assessment form; the card may be limited to 12 months.
- **Sleep apnea:** examiners may require testing or proof of CPAP compliance.
- **Vision in one eye:** FMCSA has an alternative vision standard with specialist evaluation.
- **Hearing loss:** hearing exemptions exist for some drivers.
- **Seizure history:** specific rules and possible exemption programs.

Bring medication lists and specialist notes to avoid delays.

## How long is a DOT medical card good for?

Up to **24 months**. The examiner may issue a shorter certificate (for example 3, 6, or 12 months) to monitor high blood pressure or other conditions. You must keep a valid certificate to keep your CDL privileges for non-excepted interstate driving.

## How results reach your state

FMCSA has moved to electronic transmission of medical certification: certified examiners report results to FMCSA, which shares them with state licensing agencies. Keep a copy of your certificate anyway, and check with your state on whether you still need to self-certify your driving type or submit paperwork.

## Finding a certified examiner

Only examiners on the [FMCSA National Registry](https://nationalregistry.fmcsa.dot.gov/) can perform DOT physicals. Search by ZIP code. Occupational health clinics, urgent care centers, and chiropractors are common registered examiners.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a DOT physical before my CDL permit?
Yes. Most states require a valid medical examiner's certificate (and self-certification of your driving type) before issuing a CLP.

### Is the DOT physical a drug test?
No. The physical includes a urinalysis for medical screening only. DOT drug tests are separate and usually required by employers.

### Can I fail a DOT physical for high blood pressure?
At 180/110 or higher you can't be certified until it's controlled. Between 140/90 and 180/110 you may receive a shorter certificate.

### Does ELDT require a medical card?
ELDT theory doesn't. You'll need the medical certificate for your CLP, which you need for behind-the-wheel training on public roads.
