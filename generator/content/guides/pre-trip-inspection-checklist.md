:::answer
**A CDL pre-trip inspection follows a set order: engine compartment, cab check and engine start, in-cab air brake test, external lights, a walk-around of steering, suspension, brakes, wheels and tires on each axle, then coupling and trailer (Class A).** On the skills test you must point to each item, name it, and say what you're checking for (for example, "mounted securely, not cracked, bent, or broken"). Federal rules also require drivers to be satisfied the vehicle is in safe operating condition before every trip.
:::

:::tip Use the examiner's language
For most components, say what it is and that it is "properly mounted and secure, not cracked, bent, broken, or leaking." For hoses and lines add "not cut, chafed, or worn." For fluids, check level and leaks.
:::

## 1. Engine compartment

- Fluid levels and leaks: oil, coolant, power steering
- Water pump, alternator, and air compressor, with belts (tension, wear)
- Hoses (cracks, wear, leaks)
- Steering box, steering linkage
- Wiring (secure, insulation intact)

## 2. Cab check and engine start

- Clutch and gearshift (if manual), parking brake set
- Gauges after start: oil pressure rising, air pressure building, coolant temperature, voltmeter
- Warning lights and buzzers clear
- Mirrors and windshield clean, not cracked; wipers and washers work
- Horns (air and electric), heater/defroster
- Lights: headlights, high beams, four-way flashers, turn signals
- Emergency equipment: fire extinguisher (charged), three reflective triangles, spare fuses if required
- Seat belt secure and working

## 3. In-cab air brake test (air-brake vehicles)

| Check | Standard to know |
|---|---|
| Air pressure build-up | Typically 85 to 100 psi within 45 seconds (dual systems, at operating RPM) |
| Governor cut-out | Commonly about 120 to 140 psi |
| Static leak (brakes released) | No more than 2 psi/min single vehicle, 3 psi/min combination |
| Applied leak (brakes held) | No more than 3 psi/min single, 4 psi/min combination after initial drop |
| Low air warning | Activates at or before 60 psi |
| Spring brakes / parking valve | Pop out between about 20 and 45 psi |
| Parking brake check | Vehicle shouldn't move in low gear with parking brake set |
| Service brake check | Vehicle stops straight at about 5 mph |

Values above follow the national model CDL manual; use your state manual's numbers on the test.

## 4. External inspection: lights and front

Headlights, turn signals, clearance and marker lights, reflectors; windshield; front bumper; steering axle tires and wheels.

## 5. Steering and suspension (each axle)

Steering components (no looseness), springs (no broken leaves), spring mounts, shock absorbers, air bags (if equipped), torque arms.

## 6. Brakes (each axle)

Slack adjusters and push rods (adjustment, not more than about 1 inch of travel when pulled by hand on manual adjusters), brake chambers, brake hoses and lines, drums or rotors, linings (not worn dangerously thin, no oil contamination).

## 7. Wheels and tires (each axle)

| Item | What to check |
|---|---|
| Tread depth | At least 4/32 inch on steering axle tires, 2/32 inch on other tires |
| Tire condition | No cuts, bulges, exposed cords, mismatched sizes, or dual tires touching |
| Inflation | Properly inflated (check with a gauge) |
| Rims | Not cracked, bent, or with illegal welds |
| Lug nuts | All present and tight, no rust trails |
| Hub oil seal | Not leaking; oil level OK if visible |
| Valve stems | Present, not damaged |

## 8. Coupling system (Class A)

- Air and electrical lines (glad hands sealed, no leaks, not tangled or dragging)
- Fifth wheel mounting and platform (secure, no cracks), lubrication
- Locking jaws closed around the kingpin shank, release arm in locked position
- Kingpin and apron (not bent or damaged), no gap between apron and fifth wheel
- Sliding fifth wheel locked (if equipped)

## 9. Trailer

Landing gear (raised, handle secured), frame and crossmembers, doors and hinges, tandem release (locked), lights and reflectors, trailer brakes, wheels and tires, suspension.

## 10. Bus additions (P and S)

Passenger entry door and steps, emergency exits and alarms, seats secure, first-aid kit; for school buses, stop arm, crossing arm, alternating red and amber lights, and student mirrors.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a printable pre-trip inspection checklist?
You can print this page from your browser. Your state's CDL manual also includes a pre-trip section, and some states publish their own skills test inspection forms.

### How long does the pre-trip take on the skills test?
Usually 30 to 60 minutes depending on the vehicle and state. Practice until you can do it smoothly in order.

### Do I have to memorize the whole truck?
You need to name each component the examiner selects and explain what you're checking. Most states test a subset, but you won't know which parts in advance.

### Does ELDT cover pre-trip inspection?
Yes. Pre- and post-trip inspections are part of the ELDT theory curriculum, and you'll practice them during behind-the-wheel training.
