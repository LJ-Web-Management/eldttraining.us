:::answer
**A Class A CDL opens the most jobs: long-haul, regional, and local tractor-trailer work, plus tanker, flatbed, and hazmat freight with endorsements. A Class B CDL covers local jobs like dump truck, delivery, garbage, and bus driving. Class C covers smaller passenger and hazmat vehicles.** Endorsements (H, N, X, P, S, T) unlock specialized, often better-paying work, and first-time H, P, and S endorsements require ELDT.
:::

## Class A jobs

Class A is the broadest license. Beyond OTR, many Class A jobs are local or regional with daily home time: port drayage, dedicated routes for retailers, intermodal, and private fleet delivery. Adding endorsements raises earning potential.

## Class B jobs

Class B jobs are mostly local with predictable schedules, often with municipal or union benefits: public works, utilities, waste, construction, beverage and building-supply delivery, and transit.

## Endorsements that pay off

- **Hazmat (H) / X:** fuel, chemicals, gases; typically higher pay
- **Tanker (N):** liquid bulk, food-grade, water
- **Doubles/Triples (T):** LTL linehaul
- **Passenger (P) / School Bus (S):** transit, charter, and school transportation

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the best CDL to get for jobs?
Class A, because it covers Class B vehicles too and opens the most freight jobs. Choose Class B if you're certain you want local straight-truck or bus work.

### Can I get a local job with a Class A?
Yes. Many Class A jobs are local or regional, especially drayage, dedicated, and private fleet work.

### Do I need endorsements to get hired?
Not always, but endorsements make you eligible for more jobs and often higher pay.

### Can I get a CDL job at 18?
Yes, for intrastate work in most states. Interstate jobs generally require you to be 21. See [CDL age requirements](/guides/how-old-to-get-a-cdl).
