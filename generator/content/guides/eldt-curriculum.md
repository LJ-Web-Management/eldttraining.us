**The ELDT curriculum is set by FMCSA in the appendices to 49 CFR Part 380:** Appendix A (Class A), B (Class B), C (Passenger), D (School Bus), and E (Hazmat). Class A and B theory each have five units: Basic Operation, Safe Operating Procedures, Advanced Operating Practices, Vehicle Systems and Reporting Malfunctions, and Non-Driving Activities. Providers must cover every topic, and you must score at least 80% on the theory assessment.
:::

## Class A and Class B theory: the five units

### Unit 1: Basic Operation
Orientation to the CMV and the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations, control systems and dashboard, pre-trip and post-trip inspections, basic control, shifting and operating transmissions, backing and docking, and (Class A only) coupling and uncoupling combination vehicles.

### Unit 2: Safe Operating Procedures
Visual search, communication and signaling, distracted driving, speed management, space management, night operation, and extreme driving conditions such as ice, heavy rain, high winds, mountains, and desert heat. Seat belt use under 49 CFR 392.16 is emphasized.

### Unit 3: Advanced Operating Practices
Hazard perception, skid control and recovery, jackknifing and other emergencies (brake failure, tire blowouts, evasive steering), and railroad-highway grade crossings.

### Unit 4: Vehicle Systems and Reporting Malfunctions
Identifying and diagnosing malfunctions, roadside inspections, and maintenance responsibilities, including driver vehicle inspection reports.

### Unit 5: Non-Driving Activities
Handling and documenting cargo, environmental compliance, hours of service, fatigue and wellness awareness, post-crash procedures, external communications (dispatch, shippers, law enforcement), whistleblower and coercion protections, trip planning, drugs and alcohol rules, and medical requirements.

## How Class A and Class B differ

| Topic | Class A | Class B |
|---|---|---|
| Coupling and uncoupling | Yes | No |
| Combination vehicle handling and off-tracking | Yes | Limited |
| Range BTW skills | Straight-line, alley dock, offset backing, parallel parking, coupling/uncoupling | Straight-line, alley dock, offset backing, parallel parking |
| Public-road BTW | Yes | Yes |

Completing Class A ELDT also qualifies you to take the Class B skills test, but not the reverse.

## Behind-the-wheel curriculum (Class A and B)

**Range:** vehicle inspection, straight-line backing, alley dock backing (45/90 degree), offset backing, parallel parking (sight side and blind side), and coupling/uncoupling (Class A).

**Public road:** vehicle controls (turns, lane changes, curves at highway speeds, entering and exiting the interstate), shifting, backing and docking, visual search, communication, speed and space management, safe driver behavior, hazard perception, railroad crossings, and, where conditions allow, night operation and extreme conditions.

## Passenger (P) curriculum: Appendix C

Vehicle orientation, pre-trip/en route/post-trip inspections, fueling, idling, post-crash and other emergency procedures, baggage and cargo management, passenger management and safety awareness, hours of service, seat belt and safety, railroad crossings, navigating highways and tunnels, security awareness, roadside inspections, and vehicle maintenance. BTW covers maneuvering a passenger vehicle on a range and/or public road.

## School Bus (S) curriculum: Appendix D

Danger zones and use of mirrors, loading and unloading, emergency exit and evacuation, railroad-highway grade crossings, student management, antilock braking systems, and special safety considerations. BTW covers school bus maneuvers, loading and unloading procedures, and railroad crossings on a range and/or public road.

## Hazmat (H) curriculum: Appendix E

Basic and operational hazmat requirements, reporting crashes and releases, tunnels and railroad crossings, loading and unloading, cargo segregation, hazmat on passenger vehicles, bulk packages (cargo tanks, portable tanks), operating emergency equipment, emergency response procedures, engine (fueling), tire checks, routes and route planning, and hazardous materials safety permits. Hazmat is theory only.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the ELDT curriculum the same as the CDL manual?
They overlap but aren't the same. Your state CDL manual prepares you for state knowledge tests. The ELDT curriculum is FMCSA's required training content and includes non-driving topics like whistleblower protections, wellness, and trip planning that the manual covers lightly or not at all.

### Do providers have to teach every topic?
Yes. Registered providers must cover all topics in the applicable appendix. There's no minimum time per topic.

### Where can I read the official curriculum?
The appendices to 49 CFR Part 380 are published on eCFR. Links are in the sources below.

### Does the curriculum prepare me for the skills test?
Theory teaches the knowledge behind the skills test (inspection items, backing techniques, safe driving practices). BTW training is where you practice the actual maneuvers.
